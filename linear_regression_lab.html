01 / Business understanding

What decision will the model support?

A retail chain needs a sales estimate for each store before a planning week begins.

The business brief

Use planned advertising spend, store size, and planned promotion status to predict weekly sales. Managers will use estimates as one input to inventory and staffing plans.

Prediction unit

One store during one planning week.

Prediction moment

Before the week starts, when all three inputs are available.

Target

Weekly sales, measured in thousands of dollars.

Illustrative success rule

Test MAE below $3,000 and at least 20% better than a mean-sales baseline.

The success rule is a teaching assumption, not an industry benchmark. In practice, agree on acceptable error with decision makers before modeling.

The split must match the business use. A random split fits this independent-store exercise. For future-week forecasting, train on earlier weeks and test on later weeks. For repeated customers or stores, consider grouping by entity to prevent overlap.

What can you inspect before splitting?

Check the data schema, target availability, identifiers, impossible values, and collection rules. Here, negative amounts are invalid by definition. Do not calculate imputation values, select features using correlations, or remove statistical outliers using the full dataset.

05 / Modeling intuition

What does a regression model learn?

Predicted sales = intercept + slope × advertising

The intercept sets the line’s height when advertising is zero. The slope is the change in predicted sales for a one-unit increase in advertising.

Interactive concept lab • six illustrative stores

Sum of squared errors

Ad ($k)Actual ($k)Predicted ($k)Residual ($k)

A residual is actual minus predicted: e = y − ŷ. Positive residuals mean the model underpredicted; negative residuals mean it overpredicted.

Ordinary least squares selects coefficients that minimize the sum of squared training residuals. Squaring gives larger errors more influence. A good training fit alone does not establish good prediction on unseen data.

From one feature to several

ŷ = b₀ + b₁ × ad_k + b₂ × area_sqft + b₃ × promotion

This is multiple linear regression. Each coefficient describes a change in prediction while the other included inputs are held constant.

06 / Preparation + modeling

Keep preparation and prediction together.

Some advertising values are missing. Replace them with the median learned from the relevant training data, then fit a linear regression.

MethodWhat it doesWhere it belongs
fit(X, y)Learns median values and regression coefficients.Training data only.
predict(X)Applies learned medians and calculates predictions.Validation, test, or new observations.
DummyRegressorAlways predicts the training target mean.A simple benchmark to beat.
Why use a pipeline? During cross-validation, each fold learns its own imputation values from that fold’s training rows. Validation rows do not influence those values.

Why no scaling or one-hot encoding?

These predictors are numeric; promotion is already a 0/1 indicator. Unregularized ordinary least squares does not require scaling for this example. Raw units also make the coefficients easier to interpret. Text categories would require encoding, commonly with a ColumnTransformer and OneHotEncoder.

Common mistake: fill all missing values before splitting

Computing a median on the complete dataset lets held-out rows influence preparation. Even without target values, that is information leakage. Put learned preprocessing inside the pipeline.

07 / Model development

Choose a model without opening the test set.

Five-fold cross-validation divides the 192 training observations into five parts. Fit on four parts and validate on the remaining part, repeating until each part has served as validation.

neg_mean_absolute_error returns negative errors because scikit-learn’s scoring convention treats larger scores as better. Negating those scores gives ordinary positive MAE.

Decision: multiple regression has the lowest average validation MAE here. Refit that candidate on all 192 training observations. Keep the test set reserved for the next step.

Cross-validation helps compare candidates, but selecting the best score can make that score optimistic. The untouched test set provides the final independent check.

Baseline test MAE is about $6,043. The reduction is (baseline − model) / baseline.

MetricDefinitionSelected modelInterpretation
MAEmean(|y − ŷ|)2.468 ($k)Average absolute error. Lower is better.
RMSE√mean((y − ŷ)²)2.883 ($k)Gives larger errors more influence. Lower is better.
R²1 − SSE / Σ(y − mean(y))²0.857About 85.7% reduction in squared error relative to the test-target mean benchmark.

R² is not “85.7% accuracy.” It can be negative when a model performs worse than the evaluation-set mean benchmark. Its denominator uses the test target mean; our deployable baseline instead predicts the training target mean.

MAE is not an error bound. An MAE of $2,468 does not mean every prediction is within $2,468, or that this is a confidence interval.

The illustrative success rule is met on this synthetic split: MAE is below $3,000 and improves on baseline by more than 20%. This supports a teaching conclusion, not production readiness.

09 / Evaluation + explanation

One score cannot tell the whole story.

What should you look for?

  • Actual vs. predicted: observations near the diagonal have small prediction errors.
  • Residuals vs. predicted: a cloud around zero is reassuring; a curve suggests missing structure, and a funnel suggests changing error variance.
  • Group MAE: overall performance may hide weaker predictions for stores with or without promotions. Small groups give unstable estimates.
These are final diagnostic checks. If they motivate feature changes or model selection, you have begun a new development cycle; use new independent data for its final evaluation.

Read the fitted coefficients

Reference fitted equation, rounded:

ŷ = 7.755 + 2.874 × ad_k
+ 0.003868 × area_sqft + 4.992 × promotion
  • An additional $1,000 of planned advertising is associated with approximately $2,874 higher predicted weekly sales, holding area and promotion constant.
  • An additional 100 square feet is associated with approximately $387 higher predicted sales, holding the other inputs constant.
  • Changing promotion from 0 to 1 changes predicted sales by approximately $4,992, holding the other inputs constant.

Do not compare raw coefficient magnitudes as feature importance: advertising and area use different units. The intercept represents zero advertising, zero area, and no promotion, which is outside the observed store-size range.

Assumptions and limitations for a first regression model

Check whether an additive linear relationship is reasonable and whether observations match the independence assumptions of the evaluation design. Extreme observations and strongly correlated predictors can destabilize coefficients.

Constant residual variance and approximate normality matter for classical standard errors and intervals; they are not prerequisites for calling predict(). Scikit-learn’s LinearRegression does not automatically provide coefficient p-values or prediction intervals.

Sales associations do not establish advertising ROI or a causal promotion effect. Those questions need additional design and evidence.

11 / Deployment planning

Finish the workflow, not just the fit.

Example managerial summary

“On 48 held-out synthetic stores, the regression model’s average absolute weekly sales error was approximately $2,468, compared with $6,043 for a mean-sales baseline. It met our illustrative error target. The model uses planned advertising, store area, and promotion status, all available before the week begins. We recommend a monitored pilot on real store data before operational use. These estimates describe predictive associations and do not establish the causal return on advertising or promotions.”

This saves the pipeline and evaluation metadata locally when you run Python. It does not deploy a service. Only load model files from a trusted source; pickle-based formats such as joblib can execute code during loading.

A practical pilot checklist

  • Validate incoming feature names, types, units, missingness, and allowed ranges.
  • Log model version, input data, prediction date, and prediction; protect confidential data.
  • When actual sales arrive, monitor MAE, large misses, and errors by store segment.
  • Watch for changed inputs, changing business conditions, or new store formats.
  • Agree on review triggers, retraining, approval ownership, and a fallback planning process.

For a pilot, keep this evaluated training-set model. A later refit on all available labeled data produces a different model: record that version and evaluate its behavior on new outcomes.

Workflow recap: define the decision → audit and split → explore training data → build a pipeline → compare with cross-validation → evaluate once → communicate → predict → monitor. Findings can send you back to an earlier step.

Review the central ideas
  • Training fits the model; validation compares choices; testing assesses the final selected approach.
  • Fit preprocessing on training data inside each fold.
  • Compare against a simple baseline, not just a perfect score.
  • Sales predictions are numeric values; MAE and RMSE are in target units.
  • Predictive associations do not prove causation.
  • Repeated stores and future weeks require a split aligned to the intended use.
